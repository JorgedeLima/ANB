About Us

Auckland New Ballet began as a dream — seven freelance ballet dancers who realised a shared passion for connecting audiences with ballet.

We realised there was no home for classical ballet in Auckland and its surrounding regions, and with hours of donated time, trust and belief, Auckland New Ballet was born.

Our goal is to introduce the awe, grace and power of ballet to new and diverse audiences, up-close and deeply immersive. We’re passionate about sharing our incredible art form and believe all should have the chance to experience ballet’s ability to move and inspire.

As a small and lightweight company we have the flexibility to showcase a diverse mix of repertoire and storytelling and bring our performances to under-served regions of New Zealand. We aim for our work to be immersive, close-up and unexpected and to foster connection and dialogue with new and returning audiences.

ANB Artists

Seven professional ballet artists, trained across Moscow, London, Melbourne, New York and Aotearoa, who chose to build a home for classical ballet in Tāmaki Makaurau.

Madison Fotti-Knowles Amelia Chandulal-Mackay Zoe White Isabella Guyan Kimberley Mear Louis Ramsay Katrina Casey

Madison Fotti-Knowles

ANB Founder and Artist

Madison completed her pre-professional training at the Moscow State Academy of Choreography before apprenticing with New York Dance Project and City Ballet of Boston. Until the Covid-19 pandemic she performed with Russian National Ballet. Since 2020 she has been a freelance dancer in NZ and has guested internationally with Fever’s Ballet of Lights and the Stars of Russian Ballet Gala.

Her favourite dance memory is taking class in the Ulanova Studio of the Bolshoi Theatre.

Amelia Chandulal-Mackay

ANB Founder and Artist

Amelia is an Auckland-based classical dancer and a 2026 Peacocke Dance Trust grant recipient. Since 2024 she has performed as an artist with Ballet Collective Aotearoa under Turid Revfeim, which remains some of her most fulfilling work to date. Her New Zealand credits span festivals throughout the country and include works by Loughlan Prior, Turid Revfeim, Sarah Knox and Mary-Jane O’Reilly.

Internationally, Amelia has danced with Milwaukee Ballet II, Oklahoma City Ballet’s studio company and Orlando Ballet, and trained on full scholarship at the Ailey School in New York. She has also performed at NZ Fashion Week and led an international campaign for Viltrox. She is passionate about building a place for classical and contemporary ballet within the independent dance sector.

Zoe White

ANB Founder and Artist

A graduate of Rambert School (2020) and Sydney Dance Company’s Pre-Professional Year (2021), Zoe has since worked across a wide range of projects, including multiple performances with Ballet Collective Aotearoa (The Miraculous Mandarin, Koolish Zein, FEVER/DREAM) and Black Grace (RAGE RAGE, Esplanade). A keen collaborator, she has worked in classical and contemporary spaces, immersive theatre, music videos, short film and installation.

She is consistently inspired by the independent artists of Tāmaki Makaurau, one of the reasons she has remained in Aotearoa. Zoe is a performing artist, co-producer and choreographer for Folklore.

One of Zoe’s favourite dance memories is joining the Imperial Russian Ballet’s Auckland performances of The Nutcracker at age nine as a child extra. It felt like a dream come true.

Isabella Guyan

ANB Founder and Artist

Isabella trained at Moorland International Ballet Academy in England, where she received her ISTD Cecchetti Diploma in Teaching. She joined B&M2 Junior Company in France, dancing both neo-classical and contemporary repertoire, then returned to England for Northern Ballet’s Graduate Programme and toured the children’s ballet Tortoise and the Hare. Since then she has performed at SkyCity in Sleeping Beauty, Peter Pan and Cinderella, and teaches at ballet schools across Auckland.

One of Isabella’s favourite memories is hearing children laugh and react to her as Molly the Mole in Tortoise and the Hare.

Kimberley Mear

ANB Founder and Artist

Kimberley began her training at Thames Hauraki Ballet Theatre before joining the Future Dancers Programme at Mount Eden Ballet Academy, continuing at Melbourne City Ballet and the National Theatre Ballet School. She later joined Victorian State Ballet, touring extensively through regional Australia in full-length classical ballets.

After returning to New Zealand she gained her STOTT Pilates qualifications and became a Registered RAD teacher through the Professional Dancers’ Postgraduate Teaching Diploma. Alongside teaching, she continues to perform with Fever Global’s Ballet of Lights, and is passionate about expanding access to live ballet.

A favourite training experience: dancing at Canada’s National Ballet School in Toronto while competing in the Genée International Ballet Competition.

Louis Ramsay

ANB Founder and Artist

Louis trained at the Australian Ballet School, graduating in 2019, then joined Queensland Ballet’s Young Artist programme for two years. He has since returned home to Auckland to dance freelance and is studying to be a nurse, and has been a guest artist at ballet school productions in Auckland and Christchurch. Louis finds ANB a space to continue to dance beloved classical repertoire.

His favourite memory of dancing is as a child extra in Greg Horsman’s Sleeping Beauty for the RNZB in 2011 — which he performed again ten years later with Queensland Ballet.

Katrina Casey

ANB Artist

Katrina is a graduate of the New Zealand School of Dance. During her training she seconded with the Royal New Zealand Ballet for their 2025 season of Dazzlehands, guest performed at the South Island Ballet Awards, and worked with various international choreographers. She is studying towards a Bachelor of Health Science in Physiotherapy and teaches ballet. She is thrilled to make her professional debut with Auckland New Ballet.

Katrina’s favourite dancing memory is performing Christopher Hampson’s Esquisses, staged by Turid Revfeim, in the NZSD 2025 Performance Season.

Meaghan Rowe (ARAD RAD RTS; Pg Dip DEVE) is from Wellington, New Zealand, and specialises in coaching classical ballet and contemporary ballet. She is the co-founder and co-director of The Classical Collab, tutors at The Wellesley Studios and Unitec, and is Rehearsal Director for Fever Global productions and Auckland New Ballet.

Meaghan trained as a youth under Paula Hunt (RAD Chair of Examiners) at the Wellington Dance Academy, completing her Royal Academy of Dance Advanced Two exam with Distinction in 2001. She trained in the New Zealand School of Dance Associate Programme and won the Footnote Dance Choreolab Scholarship in 2002. After receiving a post-graduate degree in Political Science and Development from Victoria University in 2008, Meaghan completed her teaching accreditation with Robyn Sinclair at the Dunedin School of Ballet and Dance, where she specialised as a teacher of the senior classical students.

Her RAD teaching qualification was completed in 2010, and following this Meaghan moved to Germany, where she worked as an assistant to contemporary ballet choreographer Claudine Merkel while also training with the Münster Dance Theatre.

Meaghan relocated back to New Zealand from Europe in 2014 and has since immersed herself in Aotearoa’s vibrant local dance scene. She is excited to join the Auckland New Ballet team for their 2027 season.

Gracie Lewis

ANB Founder and Production Mentor

Gracie Lewis is a dance artist and choreographer from Aotearoa, New Zealand, who has worked internationally for the past three years. She graduated in 2022 with a Bachelor of Performing Arts (Screen Arts) from Unitec. During the initial phase of her career, she developed an award-winning Fringe performance, Quantum Tangerine. She also conducted a 6-week research process for Holding the Sun, a body of work that investigated unique architectural zones and volcanic topographies, particularly centred in Tāmaki Makaurau.

As a movement artist, Gracie has had the pleasure of working and collaborating with practitioners across Aotearoa, Germany, London and Belgium and is part of the core production team for Te Wiki o Āhua Aotearoa. Gracie’s journey into physical practice began with studying Architecture and Music Composition at Te Herenga Waka – Victoria University of Wellington, and slowly merged into the dance sphere.

Gracie Lewis explores the transfigured human, enhancing the delicate balance between care and physical overload. Through this process, she seeks to cultivate empathy in the viewer while shaping forms that appear unattainable, extending beyond the natural limits of human capability.

The Pointe Shoe Fund

In the fairytale The Twelve Dancing Princesses, a king is driven to despair by his twelve wayward daughters and their mysterious ability to wear out a pair of dancing shoes every night by daybreak. For a professional dancer, this is more than a fanciful story — it’s a costly reality.

Every pair of pointe shoes is hand-made and customised to the foot shape of each dancer. The custom fit is essential not only for performance but for supporting the delicate architecture of the foot and lower leg. Pointe shoes provide about fifteen hours of safe wear — a professional dancer may go through one to two pairs a month, or a week, at $130 to $250 per pair.

Within a full-time company, shoes are provided. Our freelance dancers cover the cost out of pocket. In 2027, as we prepare to mount our first full season of work, that cost will exceed $7,000.
